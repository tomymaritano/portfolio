---
title: "Three.js + Sanity CMS"
line: 3D scenes that stay editable.
---

Three.js is imperative. Sanity is async. A React re-render will kill a WebGL context if you let it.

## The challenge

I wanted a 3D surface whose content was editable in a CMS. Fetching inside the scene, or letting CMS updates remount the canvas, is how performance dies first.

## What worked

Keep Three.js in its own component, isolated from CMS updates. Fetch all Sanity data before mounting the canvas — no async inside the scene. Use `@react-three/fiber` carefully (`useFrame` plus stale closures will haunt you). Memoize anything that touches the renderer.

The architecture that stays smooth is usually the less elegant one. 3D and a CMS can coexist if the canvas does not wait on the CMS.
